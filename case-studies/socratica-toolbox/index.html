Background information

The product

Socratica is a community of people working on ambitious projects together. It started in Waterloo, and it now runs as a network of "nodes": independent groups around the world that host their own co-working sessions and demo days. The Socratica Toolbox is the guide those hosts work from. It collects what past organizers have learned into a set of illustrated issues, from The Basics and Getting Started through Growing People, Superboosting Ideas, Maintenance and Demo Days, each written in collaboration with the hosts who lived it.

The challenge

Before the Toolbox, most of that knowledge lived in scattered docs, chat threads and the heads of a few experienced hosts. Someone thinking about starting a node had no single place to learn what it takes, and the guides that did exist were long, dense and hard to navigate. The content was already worth reading. The problem was getting people to it, and keeping them there long enough to act on it.

How might we turn a pile of hard-won advice into a guide that a brand new host actually wants to read?

Design cycles

Plan

I started by gathering everything the community had already written and sorting it by the question it answered. Grouping it that way gave the guide its shape: a handful of issues, each one covering a stage of running a node, so a host could jump straight to wherever they were.

Design

Each issue got a coloured cover and its own hand-drawn character, so the homepage reads more like a shelf of zines than a wall of links. Inside, every page shares the same layout: the cover and a contents list down the side, and the article itself broken into sections with their authors credited at the top.

Check feasibility

Before anything was built, I walked the designs through with the engineers to find what would be expensive to maintain. Anything the content team couldn't update on their own was simplified, so adding a new issue later would never need a developer.

Test

I put the build in front of current and prospective hosts with a set of tasks, like finding how to recruit co-hosts or how to plan a demo day, and watched where they went looking first. Their paths fed straight back into the navigation and the contents list.

Designing with engineering

I built the site alongside the engineering team, which meant designing in terms of what we could ship and keep accessible. Every page meets WCAG contrast and keyboard guidelines, headings follow a real hierarchy so the contents list and screen readers both work from it, and the long articles stay readable with a sticky contents panel that tracks where you are.

Before and after

The same advice, before and after. The old guide asked readers to dig through one long document; the Toolbox splits it into issues they can browse, search and come back to.

What I'd do differently

01

Test with hosts earlier

The most useful feedback came once there was something to click through. Rougher prototypes in front of hosts sooner would have caught the navigation issues a cycle earlier.

02

Plan for translation

Nodes run all over the world, and not all of them in English. Building the layout around translated text from the start would make that next step much easier.

03

Measure what gets used

Page visits say people arrive. Tracking which sections they actually read would show which issues to expand next.
